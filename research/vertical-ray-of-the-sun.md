# Film Research — *The Vertical Ray of the Sun* (*Mùa Hè Chiều Thẳng Đứng* / *À la verticale de l'été*, 2000)

## 2. Light and Grade (Cinematography)
- **Hard Vertical Exterior Sun vs Filtered Interior Shade:** The title names the midday sun beating vertically down on Northern Vietnam; exteriors carry luminous, humid glare while interiors are lit by dappled daylight spilling through wooden louvered shutters, open windows, and dense courtyard foliage [S].
- **Dominant Mineral & Botanical Greens:** Saturated, breathing greens (courtyard leaves, shutters, Hạ Long karst water, moss) anchor almost every frame, balanced by warm amber/ochre wood, lacquer, and skin tones [S].
- **Saturated Naturalism:** Primary and botanical colours are pushed to rich, luminous intensity without losing naturalistic shadow depth; rainy night and early morning mist sequences shift into deep slate-indigo and jade [S].
- **Camera Movement:** Patient lateral tracking dolly shots (Mark Lee Ping-bin's signature horizontal pan/track across doorways, window frames, and botanical foregrounds) that glide at a calm human pace, revealing subjects through architectural apertures [S].

## 3. Art Direction (Benoît Barouh, Nguyễn Dân Nam, Vũ Huy)
- **Shape:** Colonial-vernacular Hanoi apertures — tall wooden louvered shutters (*cửa chớp*), transom windows, courtyard verandas, and karst rock portals in Hạ Long Bay that frame views as "frames within frames" [S/I].
- **Material:** Weathered teak and ironwood, limewashed ochre plaster, glazed ceramic bowls, wet slate stone, linen, and morning condensation on glass and leaves [S].
- **Density:** Lush, tactile domestic and courtyard rooms contrasted against the vast, silent water and limestone monoliths of Northern Vietnam's bays and valleys [S].
- **In-World Graphics:** Unsourced in designer interviews [unsourced]; built from Northern Vietnamese wayfinding, kilometer markers (Cột mốc Km0), and field-almanac typography [I].

## 4. Poster & Title Grammar
- **Poster Credits:** Distribution release posters (Mars Distribution in France, Sony Pictures Classics in US, *At the Height of Summer* in UK) used unit photography by Guy Ferrandis; individual agency/designer uncredited [W/S].
- **Poster Grammar:** Split/windowed sensory portrait composition with generous dark-jade or warm-ochre silence around a luminous photographic window, serif/sans typographic hierarchy stacked cleanly along a vertical axis [I].
